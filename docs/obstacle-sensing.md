# Obstacle sensing on the fleet lanes

This note describes what a FlightRisk edge does about obstacles while it flies a lane plan: what it senses, when it stops, what it reports, how a blocked stretch is offered to the other drone, which settings exist, and how to calibrate and check it. It is written for the operator who runs the demo and for the developer who changes the code. The full design, with its reasoning, is in `.claude/build/obstacle/design.md`.

**Nothing here has been flown.** Every behaviour below is covered by tests with a fake drone, a fake sensor and fake video. The depth threshold has not been calibrated on a real Tello camera. No MAVLink drone has flown any of this. The list of what only hardware can show is under "Hardware checklist". The sections on facing a step, the sensed way back, the sensor probe and the decoder-fed frame holder describe fixes made after the review of PR #177; they are tested the same way and are just as unflown. So are the fixes made after the post-merge reviews of PR #177 and PR #170 for hardware check H6: frames judged by their capture time, stale frames refused, one colour order in flight and replay, the frame stride refused while sensing is required, the person cue in telemetry, the torch thread limit and the calibration recorder (see "How old a frame is" and "Settings"). So are the fixes of the second review round for H6: one BGR conversion per frame for detection, ReID and the sensor, a Tello edge without capture stamps refused while sensing is required, the person cue on the ground and the drone card, the decode lag limit as a setting, the 20 s hold of a lost drone, the takeoff time stamped after the probe look, and the H5 latency measured against a filmed clock.

## Why it exists

On 28 October two Tello EDUs each flew a single `forward 400` move. About 2.5 m into the move both answered `error Motor stop`, 40 ms apart. The edges landed them, but the contact had already happened: one drone crashed and the other nearly flew into a plant. The old lane path sent one long move and looked at nothing while it ran. The room offered about 250 cm of clear floor, and the plan assumed 400 cm.

The single-drone dashboard search already had a depth guard (`flightrisk/drone/obstacle.py`), but it checked once per long move and its reactions shifted every later lane off the plan. The fleet lane path had no sensing at all.

## The model and its weights

The depth model is MiDaS v2.1 Small. Its code is vendored in `flightrisk/third_party/midas_small` from isl-org/MiDaS at commit `454597711a62eabcbf7d1e89f3fb9f569051ac9b` and rwightman/gen-efficientnet-pytorch at commit `771ce082b2ce6d033f55b3d47c1f77389ad3c180`, trimmed to this one model. Nothing is loaded through `torch.hub`, so no repository code is downloaded or trusted at run time and nothing asks "Do you trust this repository?" on the console. The weights, `models/midas_v21_small_256.pt`, come from the MiDaS v2.1 release. The installers download them once with `python -m flightrisk.drone.midas_model install`, which checks them against the SHA-256 in `flightrisk/drone/midas_model.py`. Every load checks that SHA-256 again before the file is unpickled, and loads it with `weights_only=True`. A missing or altered file makes the sensor unavailable, so the edge refuses to take off, and the message names the install command. `python -m flightrisk.drone.midas_model verify` checks the file without the network.

The ground station loads MiDaS only for the single-drone dashboard search, when that search starts; a ground for edge drones (`--source edge`) never loads it. That search does not start without the guard.

## What the drone does on every lane

A sensed drone never moves into space it has not just checked.

1. **Short steps.** Each lane is cut into steps of at most 50 cm (the `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_STEP_CM` setting). The steps are ordinary relative moves; nothing changes on the wire.
2. **Nose first.** Before a move the drone turns to face it: `forward` is its takeoff heading, `right` is 90 degrees, `back` is 180 and `left` is 270, by the shortest rotation. The step is then flown as a forward move, because the Tello camera looks only forward. The drone turns only when the next move needs another heading, so a boustrophedon costs about one 90 degree turn per lane end. The turn is recorded in the dead-reckoned pose and logged as `edge_obstacle_turn` with the drone's own yaw before and after. `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_TURN=0` switches the turning off (see "Settings"); telemetry then says `forward_only` and only forward moves are sensed.
3. **Look at a stop.** Before every step the drone hovers, waits for the picture to settle (0.3 s by default, `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SETTLE_S`), and then judges a camera frame that was captured after the end of the last move or turn plus that settle time. The capture time is the video decoder's stamp, not the time the frame reached the sensor, so a decoder that falls behind cannot pass a view from before the move off as a new one (see "How old a frame is"). Two cues are combined, and either one blocks the step:
   - **Depth.** The MiDaS_small depth model scores the middle of the frame. The score is the 90th percentile of the centre third of the middle row, so a thin plant stem is not diluted by the empty space around it. It is blocked when the score is above `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_MIN_SAFE_DEPTH`. A depth map that cannot be judged is never read as clear (see "A degenerate depth map" below).
   - **Person.** A person box from the detections the edge already computes counts when it is taller than `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_PERSON_FRACTION` of the frame height (55 percent by default) and its centre lies in the middle third of the frame width. By the camera geometry that fires for a standing adult at about 3 m or closer (an estimate, not measured). The cue needs detections computed from a frame that was captured after the end of the last move or turn plus the settle time. The age of a detection is the capture time of its frame when the decoder stamps it, and otherwise the time the frame was obtained, stamped before detection and the other processing of that frame start, never the time the detection finished, so neither a slow detector nor a lagging decoder can make an old view look new. A frame the decoder marks stale is still detected and sent to the ground, but its result never feeds the person cue. The cue waits up to 1.0 s for a fresh detection, and never longer than what is left of the sense budget. Without one it reports `unknown`, which neither blocks nor clears the step, and the depth cue alone decides. Detection on every frame is what keeps it fresh, so the edge refuses a frame stride above 1 while sensing is required (see "Settings"). Telemetry reports the cue's health in `obstacle_person_cue`: `person_cue_down` (unknown at five gates in a row) and `detection_age_s` (how old the newest detection's view is).
4. **Vote.** A step needs `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_CLEAR_VOTES` clear views in a row (2 by default; 1 is the behaviour before votes existed). Every vote is its own look, and every vote after the first asks the sensor for a frame captured at least the settle time after the previous vote ended, so the camera sensor never judges the same frame twice. Views from one hover are still not independent: the votes make a false clear less likely without ruling it out. A blocked view at any vote stops the step; a look that could not see starts the votes again and counts as one of the fail-safe retries. The earliest capture time a gate accepts is never before the gate itself started, using the settle time the sensor is configured with (`FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SETTLE_S`, which the edge passes to the executor).
5. **Move, look again, sidestep or cut.** The last clear vote sends the step. An obstacle goes first to "Before a lane is cut" below, and then to the cut policy; a person and a sensor that cannot look go straight to the cut policy or the fail-safe rules. Nothing is ever sent after a gate that did not say clear.

**One time budget.** A look has a single budget, `sense(after, timeout_s)` with `timeout_s` of 2.0 s, and it covers everything: waiting for a frame, the depth inference and the wait for the person cue. The person cue gets only what is left of the budget, and with none left it is `unknown`. The frame wait ends at the later of "now" and "the end of the last move plus the settle time", plus the 0.7 s frame wait, but never later than the budget. A look that runs out of budget is `unavailable` with reason `timeout` and is handled like any other sensor that could not look.

**A degenerate depth map is never clear.** If the depth map has a NaN or an infinity anywhere, is uniform, has a 95th percentile of 1e-6 or less (no scale to normalise by), or gives a non-finite score, the depth result is flagged `degenerate` (with `degenerate_reason` `non_finite`, `uniform`, `no_scale` or `non_finite_score`), is not safe, and has the action `unavailable`. The stepped sensor turns that into an `unavailable` result with reason `error`, so the gate is retried and the fail-safe rules below apply, and logs `edge_obstacle_sensor_error` with the detail `degenerate depth map: <reason>`, once per change. One case keeps its old answer: a uniform map of very close depth (above 1.0) keeps the action `reverse`, flagged degenerate. The single-drone dashboard search follows the same rule: a degenerate map is not safe, it uses up its 5 retries on the cached result and skips the waypoint, and a map plan stops and hovers.

**The frame holder on a Tello is fed by the video decoder.** The sensor judges the newest frame in a holder. Before the review the holder was fed only by the frame loop between two detections, so a slow detection could leave the gate without a fresh frame. Now a Tello edge with a working camera sensor also runs a small daemon thread, `DecodedFramePoller`, that reads the newest frame of djitellopy's video decoder every 1/30 s, independent of detection, and gives the holder its own copy of each new frame, converted to the sensor's colour order and stamped with its capture time (see "How old a frame is"). It only reads: it never calls `get_frame`, so the frozen-frame recovery of the Tello controller is untouched. It feeds nothing while the drone is not connected or the client has no decoder (before the first frame, or while a stream restart has dropped it). It logs `edge_obstacle_frame_poller` once at start (INFO) and `edge_obstacle_frame_poller_error` once if reading fails (WARNING). A fake edge and a MAVLink edge have no poller. After a stream restart djitellopy's new decoder starts on a blank frame; whether that blank frame can be judged before a real one arrives has not been checked on a drone (see H5).

Because the decision is taken while hovering, the model's latency costs mission time and never stopping distance. The design estimates 1.6 to 2.0 s per 50 cm step, about 30 cm/s effective: a 400 cm lane takes about 14 s instead of 5 s, and a three-lane zone with two transits and six turns about 70 s instead of 25 s. These figures are estimates and have not been timed on a drone (H2, H3).

A hold, a manual takeover, a link-loss hold or a landing that arrives during a gate stops the sequence before the next step, exactly as before. A step whose reply is not `ok` (a real contact, `error Motor stop`) still raises and lands the drone with `executor_error`.

## How old a frame is: capture time, stale frames and the decoder

A frame counts for a gate only if it was captured after the end of the last move or turn plus the settle time. Before the review of PR #177 the holder stamped a frame when it arrived. With two edges, YOLO and MiDaS on the four cores of the ZBook, the decoder could fall seconds behind the video: the 1 MiB receive buffer of the video socket holds several seconds of it. A frame then arrived after the settle time and still showed the room from before the move, so a plant at 100 cm could read as the 150 cm it was a step earlier, and the drone would step towards it. Now:

- **The decoder of an edge with `--tello-local-ip` stamps every frame** (`BoundFrameRead` in `flightrisk/drone/tello_bound.py`, the decoder of the two-drone layout). One thread only receives datagrams and stamps each with its arrival, so the receive buffer stays drained whatever the decoder does. Another thread decodes them. A frame's capture stamp is the arrival of the first datagram of that frame, which is after the camera captured it by the camera's own latency (encoding and Wi-Fi). The settle time must cover that latency: set `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SETTLE_S` to at least the p95 video latency measured in H5.
- **Stale frames are refused, never judged.** The decoder marks a frame stale when it was decoded more than 0.3 s after its first datagram arrived (`decoder backlog`; the limit is `FLIGHTRISK_MAX_DECODE_LAG_S`, see "Settings"), when the decoder flags it corrupt (`corrupt frame`), and after a decode error or dropped video data until the next intra frame, because every frame until then is built on a damaged one. Such a frame keeps the cause that tainted the stream (`decode error`, `video data dropped` or `corrupt frame`), so the stale counts in H5 tell the causes apart; before the first intra frame of a stream it is `damaged reference, waiting for a key frame`. It drops video itself only when the oldest datagram waiting for it is 2 s old: it then discards the queue and resumes at the present. It trusts nothing before the first intra frame of a stream. A frame it no longer knows is stale (`capture time unknown`). The holder refuses each stale frame, counts it (the sensor's `status()` reports `stale_frames`) and keeps the last good view. A gate with no frame captured after the settle time is `unavailable` with reason `stale`, so the drone hovers and does not advance, and the fail-safe rules below apply.
- **What cannot be seen.** The Tello's video has no sequence numbers, so a lost datagram that the decoder conceals without an error and without flagging the frame passes unnoticed. The stale frame counts in H5 show how often the decoder notices loss.
- **Without `--tello-local-ip`** the edge would use djitellopy's own decoder, which gives no stamps, so a frame would count from its arrival and a decoder backlog would go unseen. While obstacle sensing is required (the default) a Tello edge without `--tello-local-ip` (or `FLIGHTRISK_TELLO_LOCAL_IP`) therefore refuses to start, with a message that names the option. One drone on one adapter works the same way: bind it to that adapter's IP. Under `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING=off` it starts and logs `edge_obstacle_no_capture_stamps` (WARNING).
- **Torch threads.** Each edge limits torch to 2 threads (`FLIGHTRISK_EDGE_TORCH_THREADS`, one inter-op thread), so two edges running YOLO and MiDaS and the ground fit on the four cores of the ZBook instead of starving the video decoder. It is logged as `edge_torch_threads`.
- **One colour order, one conversion per frame.** The Tello's decoder delivers RGB. YOLO, the ReID and face models, the JPEG encoder and `ObstacleGuard` all take BGR, OpenCV's order, and the guard converts its frame to the RGB that MiDaS needs once per look. Before the first fix the flight path handed the RGB frames to the guard unconverted, so MiDaS saw the colours swapped in flight and correctly in a replay of a video read with OpenCV. Until the second round the sensor's feeds each made a strided reversed copy of every frame, 30 times a second, while the frame loop still gave YOLO, ReID and the crop JPEGs the RGB frame as if it were BGR, with red and blue swapped, which hurts person detection and ReID's colour matching. Now a Tello edge converts each frame exactly once, where it enters the edge, with OpenCV's `cvtColor` (`EdgeFrames` in `flightrisk/edge_device.py`, through `to_sensor_frame` in `flightrisk/drone/obstacle_sensor.py`), and the frame loop, both sensor feeds and the calibration recorder share that one read-only array. A frame from an OpenCV capture (the MAVLink camera edge, a webcam) is BGR already and is handed on unchanged. The frame loop's converted frame still finds its capture stamp: the decoder looks up the frame it was converted from. Measured on the Mac with a 960 x 720 frame, the frame handling of a Tello edge went from 2.26 ms of CPU per frame (the controller's copy and the strided reversal; about 7 percent of one core at 30 frames a second) to 0.20 ms (one conversion, now also for YOLO; under 1 percent); the ZBook is slower in absolute terms. The replay script uses the same function, so a frame in flight and the same frame replayed reach MiDaS as the same tensor (a regression test checks this). The dashboard's single-drone search, which checks each waypoint with its own `ObstacleGuard`, converts the primary drone's frames with the same function too: a Tello controller declares `frame_channel_order` RGB, and a controller that declares nothing (the MAVLink controller's OpenCV capture, the simulated drone) is taken as BGR.

## Facing the step: the heading rule

The sensor is a forward camera, so a step is cleared only if the drone really faces it. A drone that measures its own heading in the plan frame (the optional `measured_heading_deg()` of the drone interface; today only the MAVLink fleet drone, from the autopilot's compass) is held to this rule:

- Its measured heading must be within 10 degrees of the commanded heading (`HEADING_TOLERANCE_DEG`), both before the look and again after a clear view. A heading that is off, or unknown (stale telemetry, before the first takeoff), makes the gate `unavailable` with the cause `heading not reached`. That is retried like a stale frame, then cuts the lane with `sensor_stale`, and after 10 s of this the remaining lanes end with `sensor_down`.
- The commanded heading of the plan frame is the dead-reckoned one. When no turn is due but the measured heading is more than 10 degrees away (lane 0 on an autopilot whose plan frame is not the takeoff yaw, or an earlier turn that did not finish), the executor sends `rotate(0)` once per step, which means "turn to the commanded heading".
- A turn the drone returns as `NOT_EXECUTED` while it still measures a heading did not finish. The lane is cut with `sensor_stale` (`sensor_down` once the down timer has run out), and the drone is not landed and not cleared. Any other failed turn still raises and lands the drone.
- With `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_TURN=0` the executor never sends `rotate(0)`.
- A Tello does not measure a plan-frame heading (its yaw is relative to power-on and drifts, and `cw` and `ccw` reply once the turn is flown), so its gate is unchanged.

None of this has flown on an autopilot.

## The way back of a resume_search is sensed

After a manual takeover, `resume_search` flies the drone back to the hold point (`docs/manual-takeover.md`). With stepped sensing that way back is flown like a lane: nose first, in gated steps of at most the sensed step length, each after a fresh clear view. The hold heading itself is not restored first, because the plan then turns to face its own next step. With `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_TURN=0` the drone restores the hold heading as before and only the forward steps are gated. If a gate on the way back does not clear (blocked, or a sensor that cannot look), nothing is cut and the way back ends: the drone is put back under manual control, hovering where it stopped, with its hold point and plan progress kept, exactly as if the operator had taken control again during the return. Nothing flies it on its own until a later `resume_search` reaches the hold point, so the lanes are only ever flown from the hold point, where the edge and the ground replay them. The operator can fly around the obstacle by hand and resume again. This logs `edge_resume_search_blocked` with the `reason` the gate would have cut a lane for, and the gates of the way back carry `source` `return` in `edge_obstacle_gate`. A way back that a protocol v2 `hold` or a link-loss hold stopped is flown again, from wherever the drone is then, before the plan continues.

## The sensor probe on landing and on a new plan

A sensor that has been unable to look keeps a down timer, and `obstacle_sensor_ok` stays false while it runs. Nothing used to clear it unless a gate happened to succeed. Now the edge asks the sensor one question, a probe sense call on its own thread, when the drone lands (`land_now`) and when it accepts a new plan that does not continue the current one. A drone that lands on its own is probed at its next new plan. A view clears the timer, `obstacle_sensor_ok` goes true and the ground sees it; a failed probe leaves it false. Nothing happens while the sensor works, and a probe waits while another sense call is in flight. A new plan that does not continue the current one also restarts the plan's own down timer, so failures before it never end it with `sensor_down`: it gets its own 10 s. The sensor's own timer is not restarted, so a sensor that is really down stays reported down until a view. A gate that finds the probe's call in flight waits for it to end (a camera has one inference worker) and then makes its own fresh call; the probe's view never clears a step. If the probe's call is still running after the gate's own budget (2.0 s plus 0.5 s), the gate counts as a timeout and makes no call. It logs `edge_obstacle_sensor_probe`.

## Before a lane is cut: look again, then sidestep

The ZBook's H1 data (2026-11-22) shows why a single view should not give up a lane and a single view should not clear one either. With the threshold at 0.77, the share of views that read clear was 100% for an empty yard, 21% to 44% for a wall at 150 cm and 9% for a wall at 100 cm (hand-held at chest height; per frame). Two clear votes, if the views were independent, would let a wall at 150 cm through 4% to 19% of the time and a wall at 100 cm under 1% of the time. Views from one hover are not independent, so treat these as best cases.

When a lane step's gate sees an obstacle (the depth cue, cut reason `obstacle`), the edge tries this before it cuts (`flightrisk/drone/lane_avoid.py`):

1. **Look again.** `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_RELOOK` times (2 by default) the drone hovers 0.5 s and runs the whole gate again, votes and retries included. These gates ask the camera to wait for a fresh person detection even though the depth cue already blocks (within the same 2.0 s budget), so their person cue is known rather than `unknown`. The step is flown only if every one of these gates is clear, so overriding a blocked view takes more clear views than clearing a step ever does. A person seen at any of them cuts the lane for a person; a sensor that cannot look cuts it for the obstacle without a sidestep. One lane gets at most 3 re-look rounds (`OBSTACLE_RELOOKS_PER_LANE`); a later blocked gate on it is not looked at again.
2. **Sidestep.** If the lane is still blocked, the drone tries to step around the obstacle, at most `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SIDESTEPS` times per lane (2 by default; a re-task that continues the plan keeps the count), `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SIDESTEP_CM` sideways (50 cm by default).
   - **Only with a known person cue.** The blocked view must have a fresh person detection that saw no close person (`person_cue` `clear`). A lane gate's own blocked view does not wait for a detection, so when its cue is `unknown` the drone first takes one more look that does wait (logged with `source` `person_check`, within one 2.0 s sense budget): a person there cuts the lane for a person, a `clear` person cue lets the sidestep go ahead, and anything else refuses it, and the lane is cut as before. So the sidestep also works with `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_RELOOK=0`. No sidestep is tried while the sensor reports its person cue down. Every gate of the sidestep itself (the sideways move, the steps beside the lane and the way back) asks for a fresh detection too and clears only when it saw no person; an `unknown` cue there counts as a look that could not see.
   - **Which side.** The side the depth model calls clearer first, else the side with more room. A side is tried only if the point beside the drone and the end of the next step beside the lane are inside the geofence **and** inside the drone's own area: the box around its planned and flown path, cut down to the `zone` rectangle of its plan when the plan carries one (placed with the zone's `takeoff_x_cm`/`takeoff_y_cm`, else its corner). The ground gives every drone a region that holds its whole path and keeps the regions `gap_cm` apart, so a sidestep stays in the drone's own region. Lanes a re-task or a re-flight appends outside that region are never sidestepped. The altitude never changes.
   - **The margin.** A re-task or a re-flight gives a drone a path outside its region that the ground keeps only the gap away from the other drones' paths, and a sidestep of one of those drones inside its own region takes up to the sidestep distance of that margin. The ground's re-task separation check therefore keeps the gap plus a sidestep margin from the path of every drone that reports it may sidestep (`obstacle_avoid` `sidestep_cm` above 0; a drone that reports nothing, or 0, keeps the gap alone). A plan made on the map keeps the same margin in its path separation check. The margin is 50 cm by default and is set on the ground with `FLIGHTRISK_SIDESTEP_MARGIN_CM` (0 to 500), to match edges set to another `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SIDESTEP_CM`. The edge does not know the gap, so it cannot cap its own sidestep against it.
   - **Sequence.** The drone turns to face the side and moves only after a clear gate there. It then turns back and flies the lane's own next steps beside it, each after a clear gate, for up to 150 cm, and flies back to the lane, sensed like the way back of a `resume_search`. If it got at least one step past, the plan goes on from the lane; otherwise it tries the other side if it may (worked out again from where the way back ended), and then the lane is cut where the drone stopped, exactly as before.
   - **A person or a sensor down on the way.** If any gate of the sidestep sees a person, or the sensor has been down for 10 s, the lane is cut at once with that reason at the point on the lane level with the drone, however far it already got beside the lane. The drone then flies back to that point and goes on with the next lane. A person cut still needs the operator's confirmation before another drone re-flies it, and a hold, a re-task or a way back that does not clear never loses the reason. The stretch the sidestep flew beside the lane is held back too: its re-flight would start at its far end, where the person was, so the ground offers it only once the operator confirms the person cut, which releases both. When the pass had already reached the end of the lane, a person seen on the way back has no lane left to cut (`not_cut`): the edge marks the offset itself (`offsets_seen`) and reports why it holds (`held_reason`), the ground holds that offset for the operator on its own (the Re-fly button confirms it), and the `sidestep_held` banner says a person was seen.
   - **Coverage.** A stretch flown beside the lane was searched along a line 50 cm off the planned one. The edge reports each such stretch in `obstacle_avoid` `offsets`, and the ground lists it as an uncovered segment with reason `offset`, announces it (`lane_offset`) and offers it once to another drone for a re-flight, exactly like a cut's segment. Offsets have their own limit of 4 per drone and plan (`MAX_OFFSET_REFLIGHTS_PER_DRONE`), so poles a drone got past never use up the re-flights of the lanes it had to cut (`MAX_REFLIGHTS_PER_DRONE`).
   - **Time budget.** The re-look and the sidestep of one blocked gate may take `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_AVOID_BUDGET_S` seconds of wall-clock time (45 by default, 10 to 300). Once that is spent, no further re-look, sidestep attempt or step beside the lane starts. A drone beside the lane has its lane cut at the plan's progress first and then flies back to it, so a way back that does not clear leaves it held with the lane already cut, and Resume search does not bring it back to the same obstacle; a drone on the lane has the lane cut as it would be without them. Every re-look and sidestep log carries `elapsed_s` since the blocked gate. The budget belongs to one blocked gate: after a hold, a link-loss hold or a manual takeover the plan meets the obstacle again at a new gate, with a new budget. The total per lane is bounded by its counts instead, at most `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SIDESTEPS` sidestep attempts and 3 re-look rounds, which a resumed plan keeps.
3. **Never land.** Nothing in this procedure lands. Every sideways and beside move is checked against the geofence before it is sent. A way back to the lane that a gate does not clear cuts nothing (unless it saw a person or a sensor down, above): the drone hovers under manual control with the point on the lane as its hold point (`edge_resume_search_blocked`), its telemetry reports `obstacle_avoid` phase `held`, and the ground announces it to the operator once (fleet event `sidestep_held`, a banner on the dashboard). **If nobody takes control or presses Resume search, the ground's orphan countdown lands it**: 120 s by default (`FLIGHTRISK_MANUAL_ORPHAN_LAND_S`, `docs/manual-takeover.md`), with `reason: "controller_timeout"`, where it hovers, beside the lane. A hold or a link-loss hold during a sidestep stops it before its next move; when the plan continues, the drone first flies back to the lane. A manual takeover keeps the point on the lane as the hold point. Every landing, requested or not, and every new takeoff forgets that point, and so does a new plan accepted on the ground, so a later flight never flies to a point of an earlier one. LAND ALL and every other landing work as always. A controller error on one of the extra turns or moves still lands the drone through `executor_error`, as on any lane move, so each sidestep adds that exposure. The Tello command retries of `fix/tello-climb-freshness-guards` do not cover the sidestep's moves yet (a marked follow-up in `lane_avoid.py`).

The sidestep needs nose-first turns (not `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_TURN=0`) and is not used on a drone that measures its own heading or flies the field envelope (MAVLink); those only look again. It is logged as `edge_obstacle_relook` and `edge_obstacle_sidestep` with a `phase`, the extra gates as `edge_obstacle_gate` with `source` `relook`, `person_check`, `sidestep` or `sidestep_return`, and telemetry carries `obstacle_avoid` (always on an edge that may sidestep, otherwise once the plan has something to report): `relooks`, `relook_clears`, `sidesteps`, `sidestep_passes`, the `phase` under way (`relook`, `sidestep`, `return`, `held` or null), `held_reason` while held, `offsets` with `offsets_seen`, and `sidestep_cm`, how far this edge may sidestep (0 when it never does), which the ground uses for its sidestep margin. Set `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_RELOOK=0` and `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SIDESTEP_CM=0` for the behaviour before this.

## The cut policy

When the gate says blocked before a step of lane `k`, at plan-frame point `P` (after the re-look and the sidestep above, for an obstacle):

1. **Stop.** The drone is already hovering. Nothing is sent, and there is no back-off, because a retreat would be an unsensed move.
2. **Skip.** The rest of the lane is dropped, and so is the rest of every directly following lane in the same direction (a long displacement the coordinator split into several lanes). The dropped distance becomes an uncovered segment from `P` to the lane's planned end, with the reason of the cut: `obstacle` (the depth cue), `person` (the person cue), `sensor_stale` or `sensor_down` (see "Fail-safe rules"), or `autopilot` (a MAVLink drone, see "MAVLink").
3. **Carry a deficit.** The drone is now short of the planned grid. When a later lane runs along the same axis, the drone flies `planned - |deficit|` if that brings it back toward the grid (a `back` lane after a `forward` cut), and the deficit is consumed. If flying the lane would widen the gap (another `forward` lane), the lane is flown at its planned length and the deficit stays. A lane is **never lengthened** and **never re-enters the cut**. A lane shortened to under 20 cm is skipped. Every shortened lane leaves an uncovered segment with the reason `skipped`, because the drone did not look there either. So does every lane that the drone, standing behind its planned position, cannot reach the end of (a displaced lane, or a lane or more behind): the part it never gets to is reported `skipped`, not assumed covered. `uncovered_cm` therefore grows, and a re-flight carries more of these shadows. The rule is a pure function, `apply_cut_deficit` in `flightrisk/drone/lane_steps.py`, which the ground uses too, so both sides derive the same segments.
4. **Report.** The edge keeps the last eight cuts as `(lane, flown_cm)` pairs, adds one to `obstacle_seq`, and logs `edge_obstacle_stop` and, for each lane it shortened, `edge_lane_cut`.
5. **Continue** with the next lane. A re-task, a hold, `resume`, `nudge` and manual takeover work as before.
6. **Never land for an obstacle.** A Tello landing into a plant is a crash. Landing keeps its existing triggers.

Worked example for the demo plan `forward 400, right 100, back 400, right 100, forward 400`, cut at 250 cm on lane 0: lane 1 is flown as planned, lane 2 is flown as `back 250` and reaches its planned end, lane 3 and lane 4 are flown as planned. The uncovered segments are lane 0 from (0, 250) to (0, 400), reason `obstacle`, and lane 2 from (100, 250) to (100, 400), reason `skipped`.

The cut fields stay in telemetry through a landing and through a re-task that continues the plan, so the ground sees the last cut. They are cleared only when the edge accepts a plan that does not continue the old one.

## Offer once: re-flying a cut segment with the other drone

A segment with the reason `obstacle` or `sensor_stale` is offered once to another drone. A `person` segment is offered once too, but only after the operator confirms it on the dashboard (see "A person cut waits for the operator" below). `sensor_down` is not offered (it ends the plan early and produces whole lanes, which is the operator's call), `autopilot` is not offered (nothing is verified on field drones), and a `skipped` segment on its own is not offered; a skipped segment travels with the cut that caused it. A cutting drone has at most 4 cuts offered per plan (`MAX_REFLIGHTS_PER_DRONE`), so a drone with a flapping sensor cannot flood the other drone with work.

- **It is an ordinary re-task.** The receiver gets a normal `assign_lanes`: its own lanes unchanged, then a transit, then the segment. Nothing changes on the wire, and a mixed-version fleet is unaffected. The receiver flies the appended lanes nose first in sensed steps with the same cut policy, at its own altitude slot.
- **Planned from where the receiver actually is.** The transit starts at the position the receiver's own lanes really end at, which the ground works out by replaying the receiver's lanes with its cuts, not at the end the plan promised. The separation check against the other drones, the check against the cutting drone's position and the geofence check all use that path as it is actually flown. This replaces the first design, which planned the transit from the planned end of the receiver's lanes.
- **The re-task start.** At the first appended lane of a continuing re-task the deficit that earlier cuts left, and any run of lanes in the same direction, end. The old cuts never shorten or skip an appended lane, and the appended lanes are flown as planned from the drone's actual position. The ground and the edge both apply this one rule (`flightrisk/drone/lane_steps.py`: the ground passes the indexes of its re-task starts as `retask_starts`, and the edge marks the first appended lane), so they derive the same segments.
- **Flown reversed.** The `obstacle` segment is flown from its far end toward the cut point, because the cutting drone found the obstacle within about 150 cm beyond the cut point, and approaching from the cut point would reproduce the block. Approaching from the far end covers what the first drone could not see. The `skipped` segments of the same cut follow in plan order. A segment under 20 cm is not offered and is shown as `too_short`.
- **A person cut waits for the operator.** A person is not a fixed obstacle: a second drone sent to fly the rest of the lane from the far side would fly toward the same person. A `person` segment is therefore never offered on its own. It is listed with status `needs_confirmation`, and the dashboard's NOT SEARCHED box shows a Re-fly button next to it, which asks the operator to confirm that the person has moved away. Only then is it handled like an `obstacle` segment (it still waits for the cutting drone to finish, and so on); until then it stays unsearched. The confirmation is the socket event `confirm_reflight` with the segment's `drone_id` and `cut_seq`, answered by `reflight_confirm_result`, and logged as the `flight_safety` event `reflight_confirmed`. It counts toward the cutting drone's 4 re-flights as soon as the cut is reported.
- **The cutting drone must be done first.** The offer waits (status `waiting`, `waiting_for` the cutting drone) until that drone's telemetry shows `lane_index >= lanes_total`, or it has landed, or it is lost. The two drones share neither a zone nor an altitude, and 30 cm between the Tello slots is not a safe separation for two drones in the same zone. While the cutting drone is still airborne near the segment, its position is treated as a point obstacle, and the offer stays queued until the operator lands it. A drone declared lost may still be in the air: a Tello whose edge stopped sending commands hovers up to 15 s before it starts to land by itself, and its auto-land descent takes another 2 to 4 s. So the last reported position of a lost cutting drone, and of any other lost drone, still holds the offer for 20 s after the loss (`LOST_OBSTACLE_S`: the hover, the descent and a margin), or until its telemetry says it is not flying. Then it no longer holds the offer, because the position is frozen and the offer must not wait for ever. The lanes of a lost drone wait in the same way when the path to them comes within the zone gap of a lost drone's last position, with the waiting reason `<id> would come within <n> cm of <lost id> (lost; it may still hover there) ...`. A drone that never reported a position holds nothing.
- **Who can receive it.** A drone other than the cutting one, with fresh telemetry, flying, with a battery of at least 30 percent, in `stepped` sensing with a working sensor and a healthy drone link, and whose geofence contains the whole new path. A drone flying unsensed never receives a segment. A drone whose telemetry says it is in `stepped` sensing but its sensor is not ok flies no step, so it receives neither a re-flight nor the lanes of a lost drone, and neither does a drone whose own lanes a `sensor_down` cut has ended (it would fly nothing of what is appended). A drone that has no fresh telemetry, or is still taking off, is not turned away: the offer waits (status `waiting`, with a `detail` naming the drone and why) and is checked again on any telemetry. Among the eligible, the one whose actual end is nearest wins, preferring a drone with no pending offer. A refusal (`nack`) moves the offer to the next one; when nobody is left, the segment stays uncovered with the reason why.
- **A receiver with obstacle sensing takes appended lanes only after its own.** The rule, decided after the review, is that a receiver that reports obstacle sensing gets appended lanes (a re-flight or the lanes of a lost drone) only once it has finished its own lanes, that is when its telemetry shows `lane_index >= lanes_total`. A later cut of its own lanes would move the path that was already checked for separation, so the check is made against a path that can no longer change. Until then the offer waits, and the wait names its reason: `waiting for drone-2 to finish its own lanes`, `waiting for drone-2, which has no fresh telemetry`, `waiting for drone-2, which is taking off` or `waiting for drone-2, which is not flying` (it has not flown yet in this plan). The waiting lanes of a lost drone are listed in the plan's `uncovered` list with `status` `waiting` and that text as their `reason`. A receiver that reports obstacle sensing and lands before it has finished its own lanes can never take them: when no other drone can either (every other one is lost, landed or not part of the plan), the lanes fail at once with the reason `drone-2 landed before it finished its own lanes` and are listed as uncovered with that reason. A silent receiver keeps them waiting only until the loss check declares it lost (`no drone left to take them`). A drone that reports no obstacle fields behaves as before.
- **Outcome.** The verdict is taken from the path the receiver actually flew, replayed from its lanes and cuts. If that path covers every leg of the segment, the segment is `reflown` and disappears from the uncovered list. If it does not (a cut on the receiver's own lanes moved where the appended lanes started, say), the re-flight fails with `<receiver> flew it off the planned path, <n> cm not searched`, and only the remaining parts stay listed. If the receiver is cut on the appended lanes, the part it flew is covered, the rest is reported uncovered as `blocked again by <receiver>`, and it is never offered again. A `sensor_down` cut on or before the appended lanes fails it with `<receiver> ended its lanes on it (obstacle sensor down)` or `<receiver> ended its lanes before it (obstacle sensor down)`. A receiver whose telemetry shows it on the ground before it has finished the appended lanes, without a cut on them, fails the re-flight with `<receiver> landed before it finished the re-flight`: the legs it finished count, and the segments of the lane it was on and of every later lane stay listed whole. A receiver lost before it reaches the appended lanes fails the re-flight (`receiver lost`) and its lanes are not handed on to a third drone through the back door. If the cutting drone is lost later, its unflown whole lanes are re-tasked as for any lost drone, and only a segment inside a lane that is re-tasked whole is `superseded`: the cut's own segment is still offered. A cutting drone whose edge restarts while its re-flight waits fails it with `<id>'s edge restarted, so its cuts are no longer known`.
- **Statuses and events.** `needs_confirmation` (a person segment the operator has not confirmed), `waiting`, `offered`, `acked`, `flying`, `blocked_again`, `failed` (with a reason), `too_short`. The ground emits `reflight_offered`, `reflight_accepted`, `reflight_done`, `reflight_blocked` and `reflight_failed`. Their fields are in `docs/fleet-protocol-v2.md`.

A re-flight that is blocked again costs about 20 s of flight and covers nothing; the cap of four bounds that. The operator keeps manual takeover (`docs/manual-takeover.md`) and can make a new plan for anything that stays uncovered.

## Fail-safe rules

| Condition | What the drone does | What the ground sees |
|---|---|---|
| The sensor could not be built (torch or the MiDaS weights missing, no `on_frame` hook, the first inference failed) and sensing is `required` | The edge starts but refuses `start_at` and `takeoff` the way it refuses a dead drone link: `nack{reason: drone_unreachable, detail: "obstacle sensor unavailable: <why>"}` on protocol v2. Landing is never gated. A manual takeoff is not refused, but no lane step is ever cleared | `edge_start_refused` with `reason=obstacle_sensor` (logged on every link; a v1 link has no nack), telemetry `obstacle_sensor_ok: false`, and `FleetCoordinator.start()` refuses the whole fleet start with the detail |
| The sensor exists but cannot see before takeoff (a dead or frozen video stream, a model that times out or fails) | Right before every autonomous takeoff (`start_at` and `takeoff`, not the operator's manual takeoff) the edge makes one probe look. It must return a fresh, valid view: judged from a frame captured after the look began less the settle time (the frame's capture time, as for every gate), and `clear` or `blocked`. Anything else refuses the takeoff with nothing sent: `nack{reason: drone_unreachable, detail: "obstacle sensor cannot see before takeoff: <reason> (<cause>)"}` on protocol v2, which the ground shows as a refused start. Without it telemetry would call the sensor ok until 10 s of failed gates, so a dead stream would pass the ground's start check, take off, cut every lane and land. The failed look also starts the sensor's down timer. The look is reported like a gate's: its latency is `obstacle_latency_ms`, and a failed look sets `obstacle_state` to `unavailable`, so the drone card shows `SENSE STALE` on the refused start. A look that saw a view clears `obstacle_state`: nothing stopped a lane, and a blocked view on the ground is judged again by the first gate. The takeoff time (`executed_at_ground_s` of the start, and the takeoff time in telemetry) is stamped when the takeoff is sent, after the look, which can take up to its whole sense budget | `edge_obstacle_takeoff_sense` (`ok: false`) and `edge_start_refused` with `reason=obstacle_sensor` |
| No frame captured after the settle time within 0.7 s, including when every newer frame is stale (decoded behind a backlog, corrupt, or built on a damaged frame) | The step is not flown. The gate is asked again up to 3 times, with 0.5 s between attempts. Then the lane is cut with reason `sensor_stale` and the next lane's first gate tries again | `edge_obstacle_sense_stale`, `obstacle_state: "unavailable"` while it lasts |
| A sense call uses its whole 2.0 s budget (frame wait, inference and person wait together) | Same as stale. The call runs on a helper thread, so a hung model never blocks the flight thread | `edge_obstacle_sense_timeout`, action `timeout` in `flightrisk_obstacle_checks` |
| The sensor raises | Same as stale; the error is logged once per change | `edge_obstacle_sensor_error` |
| The depth map is degenerate (NaN or infinity, uniform, no scale, or a non-finite score) | Same as a sensor error: never clear, retried, then `sensor_stale`, then `sensor_down` | `edge_obstacle_sensor_error` with the detail `degenerate depth map: <reason>`, once per change |
| A drone that measures its heading is not within 10 degrees of the commanded one, or does not know it | The step is not cleared; retried, then the lane is cut `sensor_stale`, and after 10 s `sensor_down` | `edge_obstacle_sense_stale` with `cause` `heading not reached`, `measured_heading_deg`, `commanded_heading_deg` |
| A turn returns `NOT_EXECUTED` while the drone measures its heading | The turn did not finish. The lane is cut `sensor_stale` (`sensor_down` once the timer has run out). The drone is not landed | `edge_obstacle_sense_stale` with `turn_degrees` and `turn_detail`; `edge_obstacle_turn` |
| A gate on the sensed way back of a `resume_search` does not clear | Nothing is cut; the way back ends and the drone hovers under manual control with its hold point kept, until a later `resume_search` reaches the hold point | `edge_resume_search_blocked` (`reason`) |
| The current plan's gates have been unable to look for 10 s with lanes remaining (the timer restarts with each new plan that does not continue the current one) | The remaining lanes are ended as uncovered segments with reason `sensor_down`, and the plan's own `on_complete` runs (land, or hover with its timeout) | `edge_lanes_ended_sensor_down`, then `edge_lanes_complete` |
| The sensor was down and the drone lands or gets a new plan | One probe sense call; a view clears the sensor's down timer. A new plan also restarts the plan's own down timer, and its first gate waits for the probe's call to end instead of counting a busy sensor | `edge_obstacle_sensor_probe` |
| Landing, hold or manual takeover during a gate | The gate runs before the claim, and the claim is what sends; the sequence stops before the next step | unchanged |
| A step reply is not `ok` | Raise and land with `executor_error`, as before | unchanged |
| A re-flown segment is blocked again | The cut policy again | `blocked_again`, never offered again |

## The `off` override and what it means for safety

`FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING=off` flies the lanes exactly as the code did before this feature: one long move per lane, no turning, no sensing, nothing in the way of a plant or a person except the geofence and the ceiling. It exists for hardware bring-up, for a camera that cannot be used, and for a PX4 drone that has no other way past the start gate.

It is loud on purpose:

- the edge logs a WARNING, `edge_obstacle_sensing_off`, saying the drone flies with no obstacle sensing;
- telemetry carries `obstacle_sensing: "off"` and `obstacle_sensor_ok: false`;
- the dashboard card shows a `SENSE OFF` badge, and the start confirmation names the drone;
- `FleetCoordinator.start()` warns, and does not refuse;
- an unsensed drone never receives another drone's cut segment.

Read plainly: a drone on `off` is the drone that crashed on 28 October. Use it only with a safety observer, an empty lane and a hand near the land key. The default is `required`, and any other value than `required` or `off` refuses to start the edge.

## MAVLink path

The abstraction is two capabilities on `DroneCapabilities.features` (`docs/drone-interface.md`):

- `STEPPED_SENSING`: the edge senses and steps, as above. Declared by the Tello, the fake Tello and the fake drone.
- `ONBOARD_AVOIDANCE`: the autopilot avoids obstacles by itself, so the edge flies lanes as before and only watches progress. Declared by `MavlinkFleetDrone` at runtime, after it has connected and read its parameters.

What the autopilot does:

- **ArduCopter.** In GUIDED the applicable avoidance is path planning: BendyRuler (`OA_TYPE` 1) or Dijkstra (2), or both (3), with `OA_MARGIN_MAX` as the keep-out distance and `OA_BR_LOOKAHEAD` as the probe distance, fed by a proximity source (`PRX1_TYPE`). The preflight requires `OA_TYPE` in 1 to 3, `PRX1_TYPE` not 0, `OA_MARGIN_MAX` at least 2 m, and with BendyRuler `OA_BR_LOOKAHEAD` at least 5 m. The simple avoidance of ArduCopter (`AVOID_ENABLE`) works only in AltHold and Loiter, so it does not protect the edge's lanes and is not checked.
- **PX4.** Collision prevention runs in Position mode only, and the edge flies reposition targets, so a PX4 drone has no autopilot-side avoidance here. It needs a companion camera with the stepped sensor, or the override.

What the edge does:

1. **Camera edge.** With `--source camera` or `url`, a MAVLink edge builds the same camera sensor and flies in steps of 500 cm, which is the reposition granularity it already stops at (set with `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_STEP_CM`, within the field move limits). It always builds the stepped sensor, even if the autopilot later declares onboard avoidance. Monocular depth from a camera at a 15 m slot looking at trees and poles has not been validated at all.
2. **No camera.** The drone needs onboard avoidance or the override. Otherwise its preflight (`link_status()`) reports `preflight` and takeoff is rejected with this detail (the problems are separated by semicolons):
   `no obstacle avoidance: <problems> (configure the autopilot's avoidance, use a camera source for stepped sensing, or set FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING=off)`
   For an ArduCopter whose link cannot turn it in place (see the next point) the remedy names only what works: `no obstacle avoidance: <problems> (configure the autopilot's avoidance (OA_TYPE plus a proximity source) or set FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING=off and rely on it unchecked; a camera edge would cut every lane this ArduCopter does not already face)`.
3. **With onboard avoidance.** Lanes are flown unchanged. A move that misses its deadline while the vehicle has stayed within 0.5 m of one spot for `MavlinkFleetOptions.stationary_s` (3.0 s by default, untuned), and the autopilot is still in the edge's own mode, returns `STOPPED_SHORT` with the measured pose. The executor cuts the lane there with the reason `autopilot` and flies on. Without the capability, a missed deadline still raises and lands the drone.

4. **ArduCopter cannot be turned yet, so stepped camera sensing cuts most lanes.** PX4 turns to the yaw of a reposition, and `rotate` waits until the drone measures the heading. ArduCopter ignores that yaw, so it would need the autopilot link's optional `yaw_to` command (`MAV_CMD_CONDITION_YAW`). No link has `yaw_to` yet; adding it is a follow-up. Until then, on an ArduCopter with stepped camera sensing, `rotate` only turns the plan frame, the drone never comes to face a lane it was not already facing, so the heading rule never clears it and every such lane is cut (`sensor_stale`, then `sensor_down` ends the lanes). The edge is not refused; it logs `edge_obstacle_stepped_cannot_yaw` (WARNING) once when it is told `stepped`, with the detail "stepped camera sensing cannot turn this ArduCopter in place yet (the autopilot link has no yaw command), so every lane it is not already facing will be cut (sensor_stale), never flown blind, whatever avoidance the autopilot has; to fly those lanes, set FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING=off and rely on the autopilot's own avoidance (OA_TYPE plus a proximity source), which the edge does not check in that mode". Configuring the autopilot's avoidance does not help an edge with a camera source, because it always builds the stepped sensor (point 1). Until a link has `yaw_to`, the only way to fly those lanes on an ArduCopter camera edge is `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING=off` with the autopilot's own avoidance, which the edge does not check in that mode. PX4 is unchanged.

After connecting, the edge tells the drone its mode with `set_obstacle_sensing`: `off`, `stepped`, `forward_only`, `autopilot` or `none`.

Nothing in this section has flown (H9). The conformance suite (`tests/drone_conformance.py`) checks that a backend declaring `STEPPED_SENSING` provides `on_frame`, that one declaring `ONBOARD_AVOIDANCE` provides `avoidance_status()` (empty exactly while declared) and `measured_pose()`, and that only such a backend returns `STOPPED_SHORT`.

## Settings

All are environment variables read by `python -m flightrisk.edge_device`. Every one of them is validated at startup whatever the mode, the drone kind or the video source: a malformed or out-of-range value refuses to start the edge even under `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING=off`, on a fake edge, or on a MAVLink edge without a camera. A whole number is plain ASCII digits only. A decimal is a plain ASCII number (an optional sign and exponent); `5_0`, non-ASCII digits, `nan` and `inf` are refused. A setting that differs from its default is logged once at startup as `edge_obstacle_setting_non_default` (WARNING, with the variable, the value and the default), and the sensor's `status()` lists such thresholds as `non_default_thresholds`.

| Variable | Default | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING` | `required` | `required` or `off` | `required` builds the sensor and refuses to fly without it. `off` is the loud override above |

| `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_TURN` | `1` | `0` or `1` (`true`, `false`, `yes`, `no`, `on`, `off` also read) | `0` is the kill switch for nose-first turns; lanes then report `forward_only` and sense only forward moves |
| `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_MIN_SAFE_DEPTH` | 0.35 | 0.05 to 0.9 | Depth score above which the centre counts as blocked, under the default `score` metric. Under `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_METRIC=floor_ratio` it no longer decides the centre. **Uncalibrated** until H1 is done |
| `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_METRIC` | `score` | `score` or `floor_ratio` | What decides the depth cue. `floor_ratio` is opt-in and **unvalidated: 3 outdoor recordings at one height** (see "Opt-in floor ratio metric (unvalidated)") |

| `FLIGHTRISK_FAKE_OBSTACLE_Y_CM` | unset | any number | Fake drone only. The fake sensor reports blocked from this distance along the lane (plan y axis). Unset, the fake sensor never blocks |

Fixed constants (in `flightrisk/drone/obstacle_sensor.py`, not settable): frame wait 0.7 s, sense budget 2.0 s for the whole look, heading tolerance 10 degrees (`lane_flight.py`), 3 attempts 0.5 s apart, sensor down 10 s, detection wait 1.0 s, person cue marked down after 5 unknown results in a row, last 8 cuts kept, a cut-reason string of at most 200 characters, a sidestep flies at most 150 cm beside the lane before it goes back (`OBSTACLE_SIDESTEP_PASS_CM`), at most 3 re-look rounds per lane (`OBSTACLE_RELOOKS_PER_LANE`), the last 4 offset segments listed in telemetry (`MAX_OFFSETS_LISTED`). In `flightrisk/drone/coordinator.py`: the 50 cm sidestep margin of the re-task separation check (`SIDESTEP_MARGIN_CM`). In `flightrisk/drone/tello_bound.py`: the decoder drops its queue when the oldest datagram in it is 2 s old (the decode lag limit is the setting `FLIGHTRISK_MAX_DECODE_LAG_S`).

A `--source fake` edge steps and senses by default now. Its sensor never blocks unless `FLIGHTRISK_FAKE_OBSTACLE_Y_CM` is set. The sensor on a Tello is fed by `drone.on_frame` and by the decoder-fed frame poller described above. A frame stride above 1 is refused while sensing is `required`, on a fake edge too (`docs/fleet-setup.md`).

## What the operator sees

- **Drone card.** A `SENSE` badge: `SENSE ok 220 ms`, `SENSE STALE`, `SENSE OFF`, `SENSE AUTOPILOT` or `SENSE forward only ok <ms>`; it also shows the look before takeoff (its latency, or `STALE` when it refused the start). A `PERSON CUE down` badge while the person cue is down, or `PERSON CUE <n> s old` while its newest detection is more than 2 s old: the drone then stops for obstacles by depth alone. An `OBSTACLE STOP` badge while the last gate was blocked.
- **Banner on a cut.** `OBSTACLE drone-1: lane 2 stopped at (0, 250) cm, 150 cm not searched`. Re-flight banners read `RE-FLIGHT drone-1: <lead> (<detail>)`.
- **NOT SEARCHED box.** Lists each obstacle and skipped segment with its reason and a plain sentence about the re-flight: waiting for the cutting drone to finish, waiting for a free drone, waiting for a drone that has no fresh telemetry or is taking off, offered to drone-2, drone-2 will re-fly it after its lanes, drone-2 is re-flying it, blocked again by drone-2 and not offered again, too short to re-fly, or not re-flown with the reason. A re-flown segment disappears.
- **Map.** Amber dashed for a segment that is offered, accepted or being re-flown. Red solid for blocked again or failed. Red dashed otherwise. The map draws a drone's lanes from its takeoff point, so lanes appended after a cut are drawn where they were planned, not where the drone flew them (see "Known limits").
- **Start.** The start confirmation names any drone flying `off` or `forward_only`. `start()` refuses any drone whose fresh telemetry (the last 2 seconds) says `obstacle_sensor_ok` is false, unless its sensing is `off` or `autopilot`. This now includes a drone in `forward_only` mode, which the first version let through.
- **Telemetry.** Besides the fields above, a drone with the camera sensor sends `obstacle_person_cue`: `{"person_cue_down": true or false, "detection_age_s": seconds or null}`. A person cue that is down, or a detection age of more than a second or two while the drone flies, means the drone is relying on the depth cue alone. The ground's fleet status carries it for each drone that reports obstacle sensing (null when the drone sends none), the ground logs `<id>'s obstacle person cue is down ...` (WARNING) once when it goes down and once when it works again, and the `drone_telemetry_snapshot` log has `obstacle_person_cue_down` and `obstacle_detection_age_s`.
- **Metrics and logs.** `flightrisk_obstacle_checks` counts every gate by action (the fleet sensor adds `person`, `person_unknown`, `stale`, `timeout` and `unavailable`). Event names are in `docs/observability-events.md`.

## Calibration procedure (hardware item H1)

The default depth threshold, 0.35, is the old single-drone guard's value. It has never been checked against the Tello camera. The depth score is relative: MiDaS output is normalised by its 95th percentile, so the question it answers is whether the centre is about as close as the closest thing in view. In a room with the floor in the bottom third that is reasonable; facing a bare far wall it is not. Calibrate in the demo room, with the demo lighting:

1. **Record what the sensor sees** at marked distances. Put a wall, a plant and a box at 100, 150, 200 and 300 cm in front of the drone and record a few seconds at each, plus a clear 400 cm lane with nothing in it: `venv/bin/python scripts/obstacle_record.py --out rec/plant_150cm --seconds 5 --label "plant 150 cm" --height-cm 100 --tello-local-ip <adapter ip>` (record at flight altitude, the slot height of 100 or 130 cm, and pass it as `--height-cm`; a ratio threshold does not carry over between heights). The drone does not take off. The recorder feeds the same frame holder through the same feeds as the edge's sensor, so it saves exactly the frames the sensor would judge, after the colour conversion and with stale frames refused, as lossless PNGs with their capture times (`frames.csv`, `meta.json`; see `scripts/README.md`). `scripts/tello_hw_check.py --video-check` only counts video packets; it records nothing.
2. **Replay it.** `venv/bin/python scripts/obstacle_replay.py --frames rec/plant_150cm --stride 5 --out replay.csv --min-safe-depth 0.35` runs the depth cue on every fifth frame and writes one CSV row per frame: `frame`, `processed`, `action`, `center_depth`, `center_p90`, `left_depth`, `right_depth`, `confidence`, `center_stat`, `latency_ms`, `safe`, `captured_at_s`. The frames reach MiDaS exactly as they did in flight. Run it again with other `--min-safe-depth` values. `--video <mp4>` still replays a video file, but lossy compression changes the pixels a little.
3. **Choose the threshold.** The target is a wall or plant at 150 cm blocked and a wall at 300 cm clear, with no false stops over the clear 400 cm lane. The design's margin: with 50 cm steps, overshoot of about 20 cm, dead-reckoning error of about 10 cm and a 20 cm reserve, anything within 100 cm must be blocked, and the calibration target of 150 cm leaves at least 80 cm after a step that was cleared. Set the result as `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_MIN_SAFE_DEPTH` in the environment of each edge.
4. **Measure the video latency (H5).** The settle time must cover the time from the camera's capture to the arrival of the frame's first datagram. `frames.csv` cannot tell it: its `decode_lag_ms` (called `capture_to_arrival_ms` in older recordings, which the replay still reads) is only the time from that first datagram to the holder, the decoder's part. The camera's encoding and the Wi-Fi come before it (the review of PR #177 estimated about 50 ms for the decoder's part and about 300 ms for the whole latency; neither is measured). Measure the whole with a clock on the ZBook's screen:
   1. On the ZBook that flies the drones, run `venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\obstacle_record.py --clock`. It shows the computer's clock as `HH:MM:SS.mmm` in large digits (q or Esc closes it).
   2. Point the drone's camera at the screen, about 50 cm away, and record from the same ZBook while the edges and the ground run as they will in flight: `venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\obstacle_record.py --out rec\clock --seconds 20 --label "clock" --tello-local-ip <adapter ip>`.
   3. Open a few dozen PNGs spread over the recording. For each, the latency is the frame's `wall_clock` in `frames.csv` (when the frame reached the holder, on the same clock) minus the time shown in the PNG. The part the settle time must cover is that latency minus the frame's `decode_lag_ms`; using the whole latency is the safe side. The shown time was drawn before the screen showed it, so the measurement errs long by up to a screen refresh or two.
   4. Set `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SETTLE_S` to at least the p95 and write the numbers here.
5. **Measure the model's latency under load.** `venv/bin/python scripts/bench_obstacle.py --with-yolo [--video <mp4>] [--frames n]` prints MiDaS latency (p50 and p95) while a YOLO11n thread runs, to see what the sensor costs on the laptop that flies two drones (H4). If MiDaS stays above about 400 ms under load, the fallback in the design is a longer step (75 cm) with a correspondingly larger blocked distance, not skipping the sensing.
6. **Record the outcome.** The sensor's `status()` reports `calibrated`, which is `false` and which nothing in the edge sets to true; it is not sent in telemetry. Writing the measured numbers and the chosen threshold into this document is the record.

## Comparing obstacle metrics offline (metrics report)

Field recordings show that no single threshold on the current score separates a wall at 150 cm from a clear yard. `scripts/obstacle_replay.py --metrics-report` compares the current score with three candidate metrics on existing recordings, without flying and without any image leaving the computer. The candidates are pure functions in `flightrisk/drone/obstacle.py`. By default none of them is used by a flight decision; `centre_floor_ratio` decides only on an edge that opts in (see "Opt-in floor ratio metric (unvalidated)" below):

- `centre_floor_ratio`: the centre's 90th percentile over the median of the bottom 20 percent of rows, middle 60 percent of columns; None when the floor is unusable.
- `centre_sides_ratio`: the centre's 90th percentile over the mean of the two side thirds. It is expected to miss a wall as wide as the frame: such a wall fills the sides as well, so the ratio stays near 1.
- `centre_floor_span = (centre_p90 - top_median) / (floor_median - top_median)`, None when the floor band is not nearer than the top band or the denominator is near zero. MiDaS returns relative inverse depth with its own offset and scale in every frame, which breaks a plain ratio such as `centre_floor_ratio` (adding an offset changes it); the span is unchanged by a shift and by a scale.

**Record at flight altitude.** The floor band, the bottom 20 percent of the rows, does not see the ground just below the drone: it sees the ground roughly 1.9 to 3.1 times the camera height ahead. A ratio is therefore only a yardstick at one camera height, and a ratio threshold does not carry over between heights. Record every calibration clip at the height the drone flies at, the slot height (100 or 130 cm), and say so with `obstacle_record.py --height-cm 100`, which is stored in `meta.json`. The report prints the height per label ("unknown" when a recording has none) and warns when the recordings were made at different heights.

On the ZBook, from the repository folder, with the recordings made by `obstacle_record.py` (`--height-cm 100` or `130`):

```
venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\obstacle_replay.py --metrics-report rec\yard_clear rec\yard_wall100 rec\yard_wall150 rec\yard_wall150b --clear yard_clear --obstacle yard_wall100,yard_wall150,yard_wall150b --stride 1 --vote-interval-s 0.35 --json metrics_report.json
```

Each folder is labelled by its name (or write `label=folder`). The output is text only. Per label: the frame count, stride, median frame interval and height; then, per metric, the count, min, p10, median, p90, max and share of None, a clear-rate sweep over a small threshold grid, and the probability of N consecutive clear votes. With `--clear` and `--obstacle` it also gives:

- the best separating threshold, with its midpoint to the next observed value, and the threshold with no false clear (the candidates include one just below the lowest obstacle value, so zero false clears always exists; it may block every clear frame);
- the false clear rate (an obstacle called clear) and false block rate with each recording weighted equally, the pooled-frame rates, and the rate per recording at both thresholds;
- the margin: the lowest obstacle value minus the highest clear value (negative means the classes overlap);
- the share of None per class;
- a leave-one-recording-out estimate (the threshold fitted without a recording, applied to it);
- the line "Threshold fitted on N clear and M obstacle recordings; not validated." The threshold is fitted on the same recordings it is judged on. Treat it as a description of those recordings, and confirm it on fresh ones.

**Voting.** The section "N consecutive clear votes (N-of-N)" is the chance that N votes in a row are all clear; it is N-of-N, not N-of-M. Votes come from resampling on `captured_at_s` so that consecutive votes are about `--vote-interval-s` apart (default 0.35 s, roughly the settle time plus one inference; 0 uses every frame; a recording without capture times is voted in frame order). A gap of more than three median frame intervals (frames the recorder refused as stale) ends a run, and no window spans it or joins two recordings. The text shows the stride, the median frame interval and the median achieved vote spacing per label, and warns when the spacing is more than 20% over `--vote-interval-s`. A fitted threshold is marked `validated: false` with its caveat in the voting block of the JSON. With `--clear` and `--obstacle`, every metric, the score included, is voted on at its own fitted best threshold; without them the fixed `--min-safe-depth` (score) and `--ratio-threshold` (the others, default 1.0) are used. The threshold's source, "fitted on these recordings" or "fixed", is printed with each table and written to the JSON.

**None counts as blocked.** A frame whose metric is None (a degenerate map, an unusable floor) is called blocked, the fail-safe reading. For `centre_floor_ratio` and `centre_floor_span` this contributes to their score: a frame with no usable floor counts as a detected obstacle for free, and as a false block on a clear recording. The share of None per class is printed next to the best threshold so that the two can be told apart.

`--json` also writes the numbers (no per-frame lists; it refuses a folder that does not exist, any path inside a listed recording folder and the names `frames.csv` and `meta.json`, and warns before overwriting a file); nothing else is written, and no image or crop is saved. Every recording is checked first (index present, at least one row, every listed frame file there), so a partial recording stops the run with a message before the depth model loads. Share the text or the JSON, never the frames.

## Opt-in floor ratio metric (unvalidated)

An edge can let `centre_floor_ratio` decide its depth cue instead of the score, so that field tests can fly it and compare it with the score. It is off by default: without `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_METRIC`, or with it set to `score`, the edge behaves exactly as before, and nothing below is sent or logged.

**Its threshold is unvalidated: it rests on 3 outdoor recordings at one height.** They are one clear scene and two walls, outdoors, at 110 cm. There is no indoor data yet, and the floor band sees the ground 1.9 to 3.1 times the camera height ahead of the drone, so the ratio of the same scene changes with the flight height. Indoor recordings are pending. It does not replace an observer at the dashboard's LAND ALL: keep one there for every flight with it, as for every flight with the score.

### How to opt in

Set both variables in the environment of each edge that should use it. An edge started by the launcher inherits the launcher's environment, so set them before starting the launcher; the launcher's window has no control for them.

| Variable | Default | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_METRIC` | `score` | `score` or `floor_ratio` (case is ignored) | What decides the depth cue. `score` is the p90 centre score against `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_MIN_SAFE_DEPTH`. `floor_ratio` is the opt-in metric below |
| `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_FLOOR_RATIO_MAX` | 0.7986 | 0.5 to 1.0 | Under `floor_ratio`, a centre whose floor ratio is above this is blocked. **Unvalidated** (3 outdoor recordings at one height); refit it for the height and the site. The range stops at 1.0 until indoor data exists |

On the ZBook, in the window that then starts the launcher or the edge:

```
set FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_METRIC=floor_ratio
set FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_FLOOR_RATIO_MAX=0.7986
```

In PowerShell use `$env:FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_METRIC = "floor_ratio"`, and on macOS or Linux `export FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_METRIC=floor_ratio`. To go back to the score, unset the variable (or set it to `score`) and start the edges again; the setting is read once, at the edge's start.

Both variables are validated like every obstacle setting (see "Settings"): whatever the metric, the sensing mode or the drone kind, an unknown metric or a limit that is not a plain number from 0.5 to 1.0 refuses to start the edge. The launcher's Setup reports the same values as an error before Start, through the same parser. A value that differs from its default is logged at startup as `edge_obstacle_setting_non_default`; a floor ratio limit set while the metric is `score` says that it has no effect. While `floor_ratio` is selected the edge also logs `edge_obstacle_metric_unvalidated` once at startup, at warning level: "floor_ratio is unvalidated: 3 outdoor recordings at one height".

### What it decides

The ratio is `centre_floor_ratio` of the raw depth map of the judged view: the centre's 90th percentile over the median of the floor band (see "Comparing obstacle metrics offline"). The live gate and `scripts/obstacle_replay.py --metrics-report` call the same function on the same raw map, and tests check that both give the same value for the same map, a 192 x 256 map and a ratio exactly at the limit included. That holds for the same map only. The map comes from MiDaS on the computer's compute device (MPS on the Mac, the CPU on the ZBook), and the same frame can give a slightly different map on another device; with a field margin of 0.0016 that can matter. `edge_obstacle_metric` and the report both name the device. **Before fitting a limit, replay one recording on both laptops and compare the floor ratios**, and fit on the laptop that flies.

- A ratio at or below `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_FLOOR_RATIO_MAX` is clear; above it, the depth cue blocks the step (cut reason `obstacle`).
- **No floor reference** (the ratio is None: the floor band looks farther than the top band of the view, or its median is not positive) is **blocked**, with cut reason `obstacle` and depth state `no_floor_reference`. It never counts as clear, and it is not `unavailable`, which stays reserved for a sensor that failed to look. So a run of such views cuts lanes with `obstacle` and never ends them with `sensor_down`.
- A degenerate depth map, a blank frame, a missing fresh frame and a timeout are `unavailable` exactly as under the score. Here the live gate and the replay differ on purpose: the replay still computes a ratio from a degenerate map (and its score is None), while the gate never judges such a map.
- The person cue is unchanged: a close person still blocks, and an unknown cue neither blocks nor clears.
- `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_MIN_SAFE_DEPTH` no longer decides the centre. The score is still computed and logged next to the ratio, so the two can be compared on the same views.

### What it reports

- **Edge startup.** `edge_obstacle_metric_unvalidated` (WARNING), above.
- **Launcher preflight.** A WARN row, "Obstacle metric", that starts "Opt-in metric; threshold not validated indoors (H1): fitted on 3 outdoor recordings at one height" and names the limit. It never blocks a start. While the inherited `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING` is `off` the row is INFO instead, because no edge then uses the metric.
- **Drone card.** The `SENSE` badge ends with the ratio of the latest view and the limit, to four decimals, for example `SENSE ok 220 ms, floor ratio (unvalidated) 0.712 / 0.7986`, or `floor ratio (unvalidated) no floor / 0.7986` for a view without a floor reference.
- **Telemetry.** No new field. The existing `obstacle_person_cue` object also carries `depth_metric` (`floor_ratio`), `depth_metric_value` (the ratio of the latest gate; null when that gate had no floor reference or could not look), `depth_metric_max` and `depth_state` (`clear`, `blocked`, `no_floor_reference`, or null). The ground's fleet status keeps them for the dashboard.
- **Logs.** One `edge_obstacle_metric` event (INFO) per judged view, with the metric, its value, the limit, the depth state, the gate's result, the score of the same view and the compute device (`docs/observability-events.md`). In `edge_obstacle_gate` and `edge_obstacle_stop`, `depth_score` is the ratio, the statistic compared with the limit, and null for a view without a floor reference.
- **Metrics.** `flightrisk_obstacle_checks` counts a view without a floor reference as the action `no_floor_reference`. `flightrisk_obstacle_floor_ratio` is a histogram of the ratio of each judged view, by `depth_state`. `flightrisk_obstacle_depth_ratio` (the score over its threshold) is recorded only while the score decides.

### Field numbers so far

Recorded at 110 cm, outdoors: one clear recording and two wall recordings.

| Metric | Threshold | False clear | False block | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current score | its best threshold | 23.3% | not reported | The clear and wall ranges overlap |
| `centre_floor_ratio` | 0.7978 | 0% | 0% | Leave-one-recording-out false clear 0%. Clear range 0.747 to 0.7978; lowest wall value 0.7994; margin 0.0016. None on 0% of clear frames and 6.1% of obstacle frames |

The highest clear value seen was 0.7978 and the lowest wall value 0.7994, so the margin between the classes is 0.0016. The default limit, 0.7986, is the midpoint between them: it lies strictly between the highest clear and the lowest wall value, 0.0008 from each. That is a description of three recordings at one height in one place, not a validation. A slightly different floor, light, height or compute device can move a clear view over the limit or a wall under it.

### Refit it for each height and site

1. Record at the height the drone flies at (its slot, 100 or 130 cm), never on the ground or at another height, with `scripts/obstacle_record.py --height-cm <slot>` (calibration step 1). Record clear lanes and the obstacles that matter (a wall and a plant at 100 and 150 cm), in the room or yard where it will fly.
2. Replay one recording on both laptops and compare the ratios (the report names its compute device). Then run `scripts/obstacle_replay.py --metrics-report` with `--clear` and `--obstacle` on the laptop that flies (see "Comparing obstacle metrics offline"). Read the `centre_floor_ratio` rows: its best threshold, the false clear rate per recording, the margin, the leave-one-recording-out estimate and the share of None per class. A negative or tiny margin means the metric does not separate these recordings.
3. Set the fitted threshold as `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_FLOOR_RATIO_MAX` for that height and site only, and refit after any change of height, room, lighting or laptop.

## Known limits

- **The opt-in floor ratio metric is unvalidated.** Its default limit, 0.7986, rests on 3 outdoor recordings at one height (110 cm), with a margin of 0.0016 between the clear and wall values, no indoor data and one compute device. It is off by default; with it on, refit the limit at flight height for the site and keep an observer at LAND ALL (see "Opt-in floor ratio metric (unvalidated)").
- **The depth threshold is uncalibrated** until H1 is done. 0.35 may stop a drone facing an empty wall or let it fly into a plant. Until then treat the sensor as a first guess and keep a human ready.
- **Nothing on MAVLink has flown.** The parameter checks, `ONBOARD_AVOIDANCE`, `STOPPED_SHORT`, the 3.0 s stationary window and the 500 cm camera step are tested against a simulated MAVSDK only.
- **Nudge and manual moves are body-relative and unsensed.** An operator nudge or a manual step is a relative move in the direction the drone faces. With nose-first turns, "forward" now depends on which way the drone is facing at the end of its lane, not on its takeoff heading. They are not gated by the sensor, and the operator is the sensor.
- **Cost in time.** Stepped lanes are slower (see above). The numbers are estimates and have not been timed.
- **Monocular depth is a weak sensor.** It misses thin stems and wires, glass, and plants whose leaves sit below the middle row. It can stop for a far flat wall or a textureless floor. The person cue catches only a person who is large in the frame, not one who is lying down or far away.
- **CPU.** The design estimates 5 to 10 percent of the ZBook for two drones, and a drop of about 0.3 detections per second per drone. This is unmeasured until H4.
- **ArduCopter with stepped camera sensing cuts every lane it is not already facing**, whatever avoidance the autopilot has, because no link has `yaw_to` yet (see "MAVLink path"). To fly those lanes, set `FLIGHTRISK_OBSTACLE_SENSING=off` and rely on the autopilot's own avoidance (`OA_TYPE` plus a proximity source), which the edge does not check in that mode. PX4 is not affected.
- **The dashboard map draws appended lanes where they were planned.** It draws a drone's lanes from its takeoff point, so after a cut a re-flight or lost-drone chunk appended to that drone is drawn where it was planned, not where it is flown. The ground's own planning and separation checks use the real path. Fixing the map is a follow-up.
- **The heading rule is not applied to a Tello.** Its yaw is relative to power-on and drifts, so the executor trusts the dead-reckoned heading and the turn reply (hardware item H3).
- **Re-flights wait for the cutting drone.** A drone that hovers near the segment keeps the offer queued until it is landed. The receiver may hover, waiting, until then; the battery gate lands at 15 percent regardless. A receiver that reports obstacle sensing also takes appended lanes only after its own lanes, which can make a re-flight start later still.
- **Mobile apps.** A cut adds an optional `uncovered` key to `fleet_status` and `segment` entries to the plan. The iOS and Android apps skip segments cleanly (they count segments as entries not drawn in `PlanGeometry.swift` and `PlanGeometry.kt` respectively) and ignore the new `fleet_status.uncovered` list and the per-drone `obstacle_*`, `uncovered_cm` and `reflights` keys.
- **The `edge_obstacle_*` events are not counted in `flightrisk_flight_safety_events`.** They are logged by name. The counts come from `flightrisk_obstacle_checks`.
- **Silent video loss.** A lost datagram that the decoder conceals without an error and without flagging the frame cannot be detected (the Tello's video has no sequence numbers), so such a frame can still be judged.
- **Capture stamps need `--tello-local-ip`.** djitellopy's own decoder gives none, so a Tello edge without the binding refuses to start while sensing is required. Under `off` it starts, and its frames count from their arrival. The launcher's preflight does not flag a single Tello without an adapter IP yet; the edge's refusal does.
- **The single-drone dashboard search** (`flightrisk/drone/obstacle.py` as the dashboard uses it) still hands the Tello's RGB frames to the guard, which reads them as BGR. Only the fleet lane path was fixed here.
